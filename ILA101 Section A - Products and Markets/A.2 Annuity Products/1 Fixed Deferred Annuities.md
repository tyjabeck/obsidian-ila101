A deferred annuity is fundamentally a savings accumulation product. While they were originally developed with the goal of allowing the PH to accumulate money that would eventually be annuitized, today most deferred annuities are used as savings products and relatively few ever become income annuities.

## **Characteristics of Fixed Deferred Annuities**
Deferred annuities are used to accumulate funds for retirement on a tax-favored basis.  
2 types:  
1. SPDA - the PH makes a lump sum deposit (most common)  
2. FPDA (flexible prem) - allows ongoing deposits into the contract  

The AV grows with crediting interest, and the insurer may deduct periodic expense charges as well.  
- Same mechanics as a UL AV, without any COI charges

## **Features of SPDAs and FPDAs**
1. Can be qualified or non-qualified  
   - "Qualified" contracts get tax-favored treatment (tax deferral similar to a 401k)  
   - Non-qualified SPDAs are sold by stockbrokers, financial institutions, and life brokers  
2. SPDAs may have minimum required prems  
3. Charges may include front-end loads (FELs), periodic fees, and surrender charges  
   - FEL = % deducted from prem before applying to AV  
   - Surrender charge is usually intended to recover acquisition costs on early surrender  
   - FPDAs commonly charge an annual maintenance charge (usually smaller than a life insurance policy)  
   - Surrender charges are much more common than FELs  
   - FPDAs may have surrender charges based on prems paid  
     - Each prem has its own surrender charge period  
     - Surrender charge = level % of prems paid  
     - Surrender charge may = % of $\min \left( \text{AV, Last X Years' Prems}\right)$  
4. Interest rates and guarantee periods  
   - Most SPDAs guarantee a crediting rate higher than the minimum for at least 1 year (up to 7)  
   - FPDAs generally guarantee the initial interest rate for 1 year (usually specific to each deposit)  
   - Minimum guaranteed interest rates (MGIRs) are limited to 1-3% by regulation  
   - Contracts have a redetermination provision that allows the insurer to adjust the MGIR in response to the interest rate experience.

## **Variations in Design of Deferred Annuities**
1. CD annuities  
   - Designed to function like (and compete with) certificates of deposit  
   - Surrender charge period and interest rate guarantee period are the same ("coterminous")  
   - PH can surrender contract penalty-free during a 30-60 day window following interest rate guarantee period  
     - Lapse rates can be very high during this window  
   - Surrender charges are also CD-like and can be expressed as:  
     - Loss of 6 months' interest  
     - Loss of all excess interest crediting  
     - 1/2 current annual interest rate $\times$ AV  
   - Pay lower commissions than other types of annuities  
     - Renewal commission levels depend on level of control company has over distribution network  
     - Unlike other SPDAs, commissions are paid on AV at beginning of each renewal interest rate guarantee period  
2. Market Value Adjusted (MVA) annuities  
   - Upon surrender, the AV is multiplied by an MVA factor to reflect the change in market value of assets backing the AV  
   - The adjustment reduces the companies interest rate risk, especially for rising rates  
   - $$ MVA_t = \left( \frac{1+a}{1+b+c} \right)^{n-t} $$  
   - Where  
     - $$ n = \text{length in years of current guarantee period} $$  
     - $$ t = \text{years since beginning of current guarantee period} $$  
     - $$ a = \text{current guaranteed rate} $$  
     - $$ b = \text{current rate being offered on similar product} $$  
     - $$ c = \text{constant factor from 0.000 to 0.005} $$  
   - If interest rates rise, $b$ rises, which lowers the MVA (and the effective surrender value)  
   - If a company sets $c > 0$, it creates a margin by which interest rates have to fall before the PH gets an increase in AV  
   - MVA annuities have less interest risk for the insurer and tend to credit higher interest than non-MVA annuities  
3. Two-tiered annuities  
   - A separate account value is calculated for annuitization and surrender  
   - Annuitization account gets a higher interest rate and grows larger than the surrender value over time  
     - Reflects future income from annuitization  
     - Encourages policyholders to persist  
     - Disadvantages  
       - Has been criticized for not being well understood by PH  
       - Administrative complexity - must track 2 AVs  
4. Non-surrenderable annuities (personal GICs)  
   - Surrenders are not allowed between guarantee period end dates  
   - Illiquid nature makes them difficult to market to individuals  

## **Additional Features of Deferred Annuities**
1. Bailout provisions  
   - Waives surrender charges if renewal rate falls below bailout rate (lets the PH "bail out" without surrender charges)  
     - Bailout rate = rate - spread, where the rate can be:  
       - Initial crediting rate  
       - Prior guaranteed rate  
       - A treasury rate  
     - Medical bailouts waive SCs if annuitant is confined to a nursing home or long-term care facility  
       - Popular in the age 50+ market  
       - Bailout provisions will increase reserve and required capital requirements → surplus strain  
       - Total cost of bailout = cost of option + additional surplus  
       - $\text{Option Cost} = \text{Avg Lost SC} \times \text{Excess Lapse Rates} \times \text{Prob of Trigger}$  
         - e.g. avg lost SC = 4%, normal lapse rate = 5%, 30% of PH will lapse if bailout is triggered, 5% prob of trigger each year  
           - $\text{Option Cost} = 4\% \times \left( 30\% - 5\% \right) \times 5\% = \text{5bps of FV per year}$  
             - This 5bps would be a component of the IR spread discussed later  
       - Most companies price bailout features assuming they will pay the renewal rate necessary to not trigger the bailout  
         - Lapse behavior when a bailout is triggered is likely more expensive than the total cost of artificially high renewals  
   - Penalty-free withdrawal provision  
     - For example, a contract may allow up to 10% to be withdrawn without SCs  
     - Some designs base the percent on prems paid  
     - Some designs roll the amount forward yearly  
       - e.g. if no surrenders were made in first 2 years, then up to 30% can be WDn penalty-free in the 3rd year  
     - Many designs require a minimum WD amount and/or a minimum required account bal  
   - Return of principal guarantee provisions  
     - Guarantees that the surrender value $\geq$ prems paid (adjusted for prior WDs)  
       - Disadvantages  
         - Increases the statutory reserve compared to other designs  
         - Agents may try to churn business if there are no commission chargebacks  
   - DBs  
     - Usually equal to AV, but may be equal to CSV  
   - Waiver of surr charge on annuitization  
     - Increases reserves  
     - May require a minimum annuitization period of at least 10 years  
   - Guaranteed settlement rates (e.g. guaranteed annuitization rates)  
     - Typically conservative - e.g. guaranteed annuitization may be based on a relatively low interest rate used to calculate the annuity payout on annuitization  
     - The higher the guarantee is, the more likely it is to increase reserves  
     - Mortality improvement has made these guarantees more expensive for insurers  
   - AV enhancement bonuses - bonuses intended to increase persistency  
     - Annuitization bonuses (common) (e.g. increase AV by 2-10% on annuitization)  
     - Persistency bonuses (e.g. credit bonus interest at the end of the 10th year)  
     - Large AV bonuses (e.g. 15-25bps crediting on balances over $50k)  
     - Bonus crediting to future deposits  

## **Deferred Annuity Pricing Considerations: Interest Rates**

### Interest rate risk (C-3 Risk)  
- The risk that rising interest rates will trigger high withdrawals and depress asset MVs  
- The company may have to sell assets at a loss to fund lapses  
- Worst case, the company's assets will be insufficient  
- Also called disintermediation risk  

### Interest spread  
The primary deferred annuity pricing goal is to determine a target spread  
$$ \text{Interest Spread} = \text{Investment Earned Rate} - \text{Crediting Rate} $$

1. Expenses  
2. Product features  
3. Risk charges
